# Writing style: no slop

Every word on a slide is read in a few seconds, so every word has to carry information. These rules apply to all slide text: titles, headers, takeaways, callouts, table cells and summary points. The checker flags most violations, but read the text yourself as well.

The test for any sentence: would a smart student say it out loud to their professor? If it sounds like an ad, a LinkedIn post or a chatbot, rewrite it.

## 1. No em dashes

Do not use the em dash (—), and do not use a spaced en dash ( – ) as a replacement for one. Use a full stop, a comma or "and"/"but" instead.

- Weak: Margins fell — mainly because of logistics costs.
- Strong: Margins fell, mainly because of logistics costs.

An en dash without spaces is fine in number ranges and periods: 2020–2024, 10–15%.

## 2. No colon constructions

Do not build sentences around a colon, such as "The result: X", "One thing is clear: X" or "Topic: detail". Write one plain sentence.

- Weak: The verdict: Spain is the better market.
- Strong: Spain is the better first market.
- Weak: Customer churn: a growing problem.
- Strong: Churn rose from 8% to 13% in two years.

Colons are fine only as labels the format requires (for example "Source:" in the footer) and in ratios or times (3:1, 14:00).

## 3. No "it's not X, it's Y"

Do not set up a contrast with something nobody claimed. This includes "not just X, but Y", "not only X, but also Y", "it's not about X, it's about Y", "less X, more Y" and "X isn't the problem, Y is".

- Weak: It's not about price, it's about convenience.
- Strong: 62% of respondents name convenience as their main reason to switch, and 14% name price.
- Weak: This is not just a cost problem but a strategic one.
- Strong: Rising costs also slow expansion, because two planned openings were postponed.

## 4. No advertising language

The deck reports and argues. It does not sell. Avoid superlatives and hype that the data does not prove.

Banned words: *revolutionary, game-changing, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, world-class, best-in-class, unparalleled, unprecedented, seamless, powerful, innovative, transformative, next-level, disruptive* (unless it is a defined term in the material), *exciting, amazing, incredible, remarkable, stunning*.

- Weak: The app offers a seamless, innovative experience.
- Strong: Users complete a booking in 3 taps, against 7 at the market leader.

## 5. No buzzwords

Banned words: *leverage, synergy, unlock, empower, elevate, supercharge, harness, foster, drive* (in the sense of "drive growth" without saying how), *holistic, robust, dynamic, ever-evolving, paradigm shift, going forward, at the end of the day, value at stake, journey, landscape, realm, ecosystem* (unless it is the literal term), *delve, navigate* (unless literal), *pivotal, crucial, vital, key* (as filler), *in today's fast-paced world*.

Replace each with what actually happens.

- Weak: The partnership unlocks synergies across the value chain.
- Strong: The partnership cuts delivery costs by sharing one warehouse.

## 6. No odd sentence shapes

- **No rhetorical questions** on slides. Answer the question instead.
- **No fragments for drama**, such as "Big market. Small players. Huge chance."
- **No triplets for rhythm.** Lists of three are fine when there are three things, not as a habit.
- **No "Here's why", "Let's dive in", "The bottom line?"** or any other speaker phrase.
- **No exclamation marks.**
- **No emoji.**

## 7. Plain and specific

- Use the shortest common word: *use*, not *utilise*. Write *help*, not *facilitate*, and *about*, not *approximately* (unless precision matters).
- Name the actor: *Customers cancel*, not *Cancellations are observed*.
- Prefer a number to an adjective: *+18%*, not *strong growth*.
- Prefer a concrete noun to an abstraction: *delivery time*, not *operational performance*.
- One idea per sentence.

## Quick check before building

1. Search the text for "—", " – ", ":", "not just", "not only", "it's not", "isn't".
2. Search it for the banned words above.
3. Read every title aloud. If it sounds like an ad or a keynote, rewrite it.
