# Action titles

Read this file before writing any title. Titles are where decks most often fail, and Claude makes the same mistakes again and again: it labels topics, it describes the chart, it states truisms and it claims more than the data shows.

## Definition

An action title is **one full sentence that states the single conclusion the audience should take from the slide**. The exhibit below it proves exactly that sentence, nothing more and nothing less.

If someone reads only the titles of the deck, from top to bottom, they know the whole story.

## The ladder

Every title sits somewhere on this ladder. The target is the **insight** level. Go up to **implication** only when the material supports it, which is usually on synthesis and recommendation slides.

| Level | Example | Use it? |
|---|---|---|
| Topic | Customer churn | No. This is a label. |
| Description | Churn by customer tenure, 2024 | No. This belongs in the exhibit header. |
| Observation | Churn is highest among new customers | Not yet. It is vague. |
| **Insight** | **Two thirds of churned customers leave within their first 90 days** | **Yes. This is the default.** |
| Implication | Fixing onboarding would address two thirds of all churn | Only if the material supports the link. |

## Rules

1. **A full sentence with a verb.** No topic labels, no "Topic: detail" labels and no questions. A question can be the tracker (the small label above the title), but the title must answer it.
2. **It contains the "so what".** If a reader can still ask "so what?", the title is not finished.
3. **The slide proves it.** Every number in the title appears on the slide. The title claims nothing that the exhibit does not show. This is called vertical logic.
4. **Concrete and quantified** wherever possible, with direction, size and a point of comparison. Write "9 pts above the peer median", not "high margin".
5. **One message.** A single "but" that sets up a tension is fine. Three facts joined with "and" are not.
6. **Short.** Aim for 10 to 20 words and never more than two lines. The checker warns above 100 characters and fails above 130.
7. **Specific.** If the title would fit another company or another project just as well, it is too generic.
8. **The verb matches the evidence.**
   - Hard data (accounts, official statistics, full datasets): *is, grew, fell, accounts for*.
   - A survey or a sample: *X% of respondents say, indicates*.
   - A few interviews or a small sample: *suggests, interviewees describe*.
   - A correlation is not a cause. Do not write *drives, causes, leads to* unless the material shows causality.
9. **Plain English, no slop.** Write as a smart student, not as a consultant or an ad. Follow `writing-style.md`: no em dashes, no colon constructions, no "it's not X, it's Y", no buzzwords such as *leverage, synergies, unlock, holistic, robust*, and no advertising words such as *seamless, innovative, game-changing*.
10. **The horizontal test.** The titles read in order form the summary. Each title follows from the one before it, and the summary slide repeats them in compressed form.

### Forbidden patterns (the checker flags them)

- Labels: *Overview, Introduction, Background, Analysis, Results, Key findings, Market analysis, Recommendations, Next steps, Methodology, Conclusion*.
- Chart narration: *The chart shows..., This slide..., Figure..., Overview of..., Analysis of...*
- Empty phrases: *the following, plays an important/key role, is important, varies, various factors, could potentially*.
- Questions as titles.

### Slides that do not need an action title

The title slide, the agenda, section dividers and the source appendix carry plain labels. **The summary slide does need one:** its title is the core message of the deck.

## Typical mistakes, with fixes

The numbers in these examples are made up and only illustrate the pattern.

| Mistake | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| Topic instead of message | Market overview Portugal | Portugal's plant-based dairy market grew 14% a year since 2020, twice as fast as dairy overall |
| Label with a colon | Survey results: employee satisfaction | Satisfaction fell most among junior staff (−18 pts), while senior staff stayed stable |
| Narrating the chart | The chart shows revenue by segment, 2019–2024 | Services now make up 41% of revenue, up from 22% in 2019 |
| Truism | Digitalisation is transforming retail banking | At Bank X, mobile already handles 70% of retail transactions, and branch visits have halved since 2019 |
| Invented causality | The loyalty programme increased spend by 12% | Loyalty members spend 12% more per visit than non-members |
| Vague and hedged | Logistics costs could potentially play an important role | Logistics is the largest cost block at 34% of COGS |
| No point of comparison | Company A has high margins | Company A's 24% EBIT margin is 9 pts above the peer median |
| Overloaded | Revenue grew 8%, EBITDA margin fell to 11%, headcount rose by 300 and NPS improved | Revenue grew 8%, but margin fell 3 pts to 11% |
| Empty recommendation slide | Recommendations | Enter Spain first through e-commerce and add stores once online share passes 5% |
| Methodology without a message | Methodology | Our findings rest on 412 survey responses and 12 interviews across 3 markets |
| Question as title | Why should we care about churn? | Churn costs €2.1M in lost revenue a year, more than the entire marketing budget |
| Consultant voice | Leveraging omnichannel synergies unlocks significant value at stake | Customers who buy both online and in store spend 2.3 times as much as store-only customers |
| Claims more than the data | Gen Z rejects fast fashion | 6 of our 9 Gen Z interviewees say they now buy mostly second-hand |

## Examples from real consulting decks

These rewrites use the actual numbers from the slides.

| Weak original | Better |
|---|---|
| Survey results on use case prioritization aligns with CRO expectation in October 2023 | Already in 2023, CROs ranked portfolio monitoring and early warning as their top GenAI area (58%) |
| Institutions leading GenAI development are typically taking the following actions | GenAI leaders build foundations first (regulator dialogue, a central CoE, MLOps infrastructure) and start with low-risk pilots |
| Why should leaders pay attention? *(subline: Cloud has already effected change across industries and will remain an important tech disruption)* | Cloud could unlock ~$1 trillion in EBITDA for Fortune 500 firms by 2030, but security remains a top concern for ~75% of enterprises |

The last example deliberately says *remains a top concern* and not *holds back*. The slide shows that security is a top concern. It does not show that it stops anyone.

## Titles when the team still decides

When the material does not settle the conclusion, the title states what **is** settled and the open judgement goes in a team-input box.

- Weak: *We should enter the German market*, when nothing in the material compares markets.
- Better: *Germany is the largest market at €1.2B, but has the most entrenched competitors*, with a team-input box: `[TEAM: Is size worth the competitive risk? Add your view from the case discussion.]`

## Checklist for every title

Go through all six questions for every content slide before building.

1. **So what?** Can a reader still ask it? If yes, go one rung up the ladder.
2. **Proof.** Does the exhibit prove exactly this sentence, and does every number in it appear on the slide?
3. **Specific.** Would the title fit a different company or project? If yes, add the detail that makes it unique.
4. **One message.** Does it contain more than one claim? If yes, cut or split it.
5. **Evidence strength.** Does the verb match the evidence? Is causality claimed where the material only shows a correlation?
6. **Story.** Does the title follow from the previous one, and does it read naturally when the titles are listed alone?
